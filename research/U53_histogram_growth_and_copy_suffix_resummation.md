# U53 — Histogram growth, exact copy-suffix summation, and sample refinement

Date: 2026-09-15. Status: self-contained method-specific growth theorem and constructive resummation/sampling identities, with bounded independent checks. No P-versus-NP solution, general counting lower bound, novelty claim or formal/expert certification.
Dependencies: U52's model and exact recurrence. All counting arguments below are proved directly.

## 1. Three distinct questions

The U52 quotient is exact. We now test its size, whether the size is necessarily an obstacle to computing a scalar count, and whether a previously merged state can be updated when the anti-checker adds a new example.

Results: the explicit histogram DP has 2^Omega(s) reachable states already at q=O(log s) examples; nevertheless its copy-only subfamily can be summed and sampled in polynomial time; and individual old-equivalent prefixes need not have one common refined histogram after another example is added. Each statement is narrower than hardness of all consistent-circuit sampling.

## 2. Explicit exponentially many reachable histograms

For any integer s>=8, set

    q=floor(log_2(s/2)), D=2^q,

and use any arity n>=q. Take the q examples to be e_1,...,e_q, the first q unit vectors in {0,1}^n, all with label zero. Initial sample columns include every q-bit unit vector, zero, and all ones. Extra input variables have zero columns.

Because q>=2, the initial distinct columns number q+2. In exactly

    B=D-q-2

gates, make every other q-bit column available. Enumerate masks c in increasing integer order; whenever c is missing, take its lowest set bit e_i and c-e_i, already available, and OR their signals. The result is c. The constants, unit vectors and all-ones column need no gate. Every such gate is an allowed B2 gate.

Next use D gates that merely copy an available column. To copy column a, choose a signal with that column as the first predecessor and use the fixed projection gate g(u,v)=u; any second predecessor is allowed. Since every column is available, after D copies every weak composition delta of D into D parts can occur as the vector of added multiplicities. Hence there are exactly

    binom(2D-1,D-1)

different histograms from these particular extensions of the fixed bootstrap.

The total B+D=2D-q-2 is at most s. Pad the remaining s-(B+D) slots by copies of zero. Adding the same number of zeros preserves distinctness, so these are also different terminal states in the exact-s DP. Each state has a positive terminal count for target y=0, since the original zero signal remains available. Thus removing zero-count states does not eliminate this family.

For D>=2,

    binom(2D-1,D-1) = product_(i=1)^(D-1) (D+i)/i >= 2^(D-1).

As D>s/4, the number is at least 2^(s/4-1). This proves a 2^Omega(s) lower bound on **explicit state enumeration by this recurrence**, not on arbitrary algorithms computing the same count.

Even identifying states related by permutations of the q example rows leaves at least binom(2D-1,D-1)/q! classes. Since log_2(q!)=O(q log q)=o(s), that more limited symmetry quotient still has exponentially many classes. No claim is made about every possible algebraic or semantic compression.

At s=floor(N^beta/(10 log_2 N)), n=log_2 N, fixed 0<beta<1, the required n>=q holds eventually and the explicit-state bound is superpolynomial in N. The sample labels are not asserted to follow the particular constant-shrink policy in U49. They also do not assert that the entire target is easy: for sufficiently large n, counting 2^(N-q) completions of these labels against 2^O(S log(n+S)) size-S descriptions shows they can extend to a target harder than S=N^beta. This existence observation does not efficiently construct that target or establish a particular promised history.

## 3. Why this cannot be promoted to a scalar-count lower bound

The exponential family just used is generated by copy gates. We can actually sum **all** such histories without enumerating their histograms.

Let M be an initial histogram with k>=1 available signals and positive multiplicities m_c on D distinct columns. Restrict each of r future gate types to the syntactic projection g(u,v)=u; its predecessor indices remain ordered and arbitrary, including repetition. The final output index is still part of the description. Write

    (k)_r = k(k+1)...(k+r-1), with (k)_0=1.

There are (k)_r^2 copy-only gate lists. For a specified addition histogram delta with sum r, its list multiplicity is

    [r! / product_c delta_c!] * product_c (m_c)_(delta_c) * (k)_r.

### Direct counting proof

Choose the chronological sequence of copied columns: its multiplicity with counts delta is multinomial(r;delta). Each time column c is copied the number of eligible first indices rises by one, giving (m_c)_(delta_c). At each step j there are k+j choices of the unused second predecessor, producing (k)_r. Different chronological choices and indices give different descriptions, and every copy history has exactly one such representation. QED.

Summing those terms gives (k)_r^2 simply because each successive gate has (k+j)^2 choices. For labels y, the exact number including output choices is

    Hcopy_r(M,y) = (k)_r^2 * (k+r) * m_y / k.

If m_y=0 the answer is zero. For a proof otherwise, under the uniform prior on copy gate lists the first predecessor is a uniform existing signal. Let m_y(j) be its target multiplicity after j gates. Then

    E[m_y(j+1)/(k+j+1) | M_j] = m_y(j)/(k+j).

Thus E[m_y(r)]=(k+r)m_y/k. Multiplying by the number of lists counts all compatible output indices. No independence between steps is assumed. The expression is integral; r=0 gives m_y directly, and for r>=1 factors of k in (k)_r^2 cancel the denominator.

This count can be calculated with r multiplications of O(r log(k+r+2))-bit integers, rather than binom(r+D-1,D-1) states. Large state space alone therefore does not prove large computation time, even in the exact family that witnesses Section 2.

## 4. A count-free exact conditional sampler for the copy family

There is an even more direct sampler when m_y>0. At a current state with k signals and m=m_y target signals:

1. Choose the first physical predecessor index i with weight m+1 when its column is y, and weight m otherwise. The total weight is (k+1)m.
2. Choose the second predecessor independently and uniformly among k indices.
3. Append the copied signal and repeat until all copy gates are produced.
4. Choose the output uniformly among the remaining target-valued signal indices.

These are small integer choices. The algorithm needs no exponential DP, global rejection from raw descriptions, or counter for general B2 circuits.

To prove exactness, let r>=1 remain. The number of valid suffixes after a particular first-gate pair whose first column is c is

    (k+1)_(r-1)^2 * (k+r) * (m + [c=y])/(k+1).

Dividing by Hcopy_r(M,y) gives the pair probability

    (m+[c=y]) / [k(k+1)m],

which is exactly the rule above. Induct over the remaining gates and use uniform terminal output selection. Every complete compatible raw description receives probability 1/Hcopy_r(M,y).

A fair-bit integer generator has acceptance at least 1/2; choosing by cumulative integer weights and retaining signal columns gives expected polynomial bit cost in k+r and q. Copying pointers rather than duplicating long column strings can save storage, but is not needed for polynomiality. This is a special-family sampler, not a proposed sampler for all B2 continuations.

## 5. Fixed-sample equivalence does not uniquely refine

U52 may discard syntax only while the example list is fixed. Let n=2, initial sample X=(00), and consider one-gate prefixes AND(x_1,x_2) and OR(x_1,x_2). Both new signals have column zero on the old sample. Including constants and inputs, both histograms are

    [0,0,0,0,1].

Append example 10, so columns now have two bits. In the code's integer convention the AND-prefix histogram becomes [0,0,0,2,3], while the OR-prefix becomes [0,0,2,2,3]. The target column (0,1), integer 2, has terminal multiplicity respectively one and two.

Thus no deterministic rule taking just this old histogram, the old sample and the new point can produce the refined histogram of **each** individual prefix. Evaluating one chosen representative and assigning its new values to all merged prefixes is invalid.

This does not rule out a weighted splitting distribution computed using additional information, nor does it contradict U52's counting theorem. A counter can be rebuilt on the enlarged sample, a richer state may be maintained, or a separately computed splitting kernel may be used. Their costs cannot be omitted merely because the previous sample had already been processed. The sampler retains its chosen physical description, so evaluating that one description on a new example is not blocked by this example.

## 6. Executed checks and limits

TypeScript constructs the OR bootstrap and explicitly aggregates all copy histograms at q=1,2,3, using r=2^q copies. The q=1 case is a boundary check with a repeated initial column, not the q>=2 proof template. State counts are 3,35,6435 and agree with binom(r+D-1,D-1). The q=3 case has 67,319,076,464,640,000 raw copy gate lists, computed as exact weights; those lists were **not** individually enumerated. All output counts equal the formula in Section 3.

Python independently enumerates weak compositions and computes their multinomial/rising-factorial weights, instead of copying the forward recurrence. It also tests an unequal-multiplicity case (3,2,1), r=5. Wolfram independently checks three such summations and all state-bound inequalities for s=8..128. These checks illustrate and audit the all-parameter proofs; they do not replace them.

The conditional rule of Section 4 was completely expanded on six small cases. There are **3,865** output-tagged descriptions in total (1440,480,600,1200,144,1). TypeScript verifies the exact unreduced integer probability of every leaf; Python independently uses Fraction and normalized weights. Every leaf has the predicted common probability and the leaf count equals Hcopy. No frequency estimate is used.

Both implementations verify the sample-refinement counterexample. Full source, domains, arithmetic conventions and actual outputs accompany this note. No claim of formal or external expert verification is made.

## 7. Research consequence and next question

The exact multiset counter is a valid positive substitute for rejection in the fixed-example regime. Its direct implementation does not remain polynomial at growing example counts. However the copy suffix admits a genuine polynomial resummation and direct sampler, so an argument that equates reachable histograms with unavoidable work is refuted by an explicit construction.

The next useful target is not another larger finite gate budget. Classify the transition operations that admit such closed conditional counts while preserving syntax weights, and test whether a nontrivial part of unrestricted B2 can be aggregated as well. In particular, a proposal to compress into low-order moments must be checked for **closure under another gate**, not just for predicting one next-gate distribution. If that hierarchy fails to close, preserve the exact failure and keep the missing link to general-circuit magnification visible.

No general-circuit lower bound, polynomial-time SAT algorithm, or complete P-versus-NP candidate has been established. All new bounds in this note concern the declared representation or restricted sampler; old results are not silently reclassified.
